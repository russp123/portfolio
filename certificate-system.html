Training & Certificate System

Replacing two subscriptions with a system they own

A US offshore safety training provider ran its certificate work on two subscriptions: one for training records and one for producing certificates. I replaced both with a single system on their own server, and their staff moved their daily work onto it before the next renewal.

The problem

Every class was entered twice. The same course, dates and names went into the records system, then again into the certificate program, by hand. Both lived inside someone else's account, so the data, the designs and the renewal date were all out of their hands.

The certificates themselves could not change. Their clients already hold them, so whatever replaced the old program had to produce the same documents.

What I built

One system, with four parts that share the same records. The Overview is where the day starts: what is in date, what is due, and what is ready to issue.

Records

A person is entered once and keeps one permanent student number, assigned automatically. Their whole training history sits on their record, and a certificate that has lapsed shows in red.

If somebody types details that match a person already on file, the form says who, with their number, before anything is saved. It warns and does not block, because two people really can share a name and a birthday.

Certificates

A whole class is issued in one action. Where a course has more than one design in use, a person chooses and the system never guesses, and everything can be combined into one file for checking before it goes out.

A certificate from the real renderer, with a fictional trainee. The watermark and signatures are placeholders here, so the real document looks richer.

Delivery record

Each certificate records when it was emailed, when the original was sent and when the client confirmed it arrived. Nothing on this screen sends anything. It is the record of what went out.

A certificate that went out with a mistake goes through Correct. It keeps its number, keeps a copy of the document that already went out, and records what was wrong.

Design editor

Staff can change a design's wording with the page redrawing beside them. Copying a close design is the usual way to start a new one, so a copy marks every line that still carries the previous course's wording.

The hard part

The old program's output was the specification, so the new renderer had to be measured against it, pixel by pixel, instead of judged by eye.

327 of 146,157 pixels

On the reference design, 99.78% of the new renderer's ink lands within one pixel of the old system's output. Most of the remaining difference is two thin lines in the signature area.

I measured it again on the final build, and the number has not moved.

What they ended up with

  • One system on their own server and domain, holding records, certificates, the delivery record and the designs.
  • 179 certificate designs, rebuilt from a legacy library of 1,044.
  • 150 of the 155 courses on their list able to produce a certificate.
  • Their staff entering real records in it since September 25, and both old subscriptions cancelled.
  • A plain-English manual for their staff, and handover documents for whoever looks after it next.

How it was run

I worked directly with their team on regular calls, and when their staff started using it, what they reported went to the front of the queue. Inside their old systems I only ever read; nothing there was changed. Nearly 600 automated tests guard the work.
